# Final Pitch Presentation (Polished)

Title: AI in the Classroom: Learning or Shortcut?
Duration: 3:00 (target)

Slide-by-slide breakdown (for a single-screen/slide or 6-slide deck):

Slide 1 — Opening (0:00–0:20)
- Visual: Hook image (student at laptop with chatbot on screen) or 5s clip of typing.
- On-screen text: "AI in the Classroom: Learning or Shortcut?"
- Speaker notes: Open with the optional hook line: "Every time we open a laptop, we are one chat prompt away from instant information. But in education, the bigger question is whether it helps us learn to think for ourselves." Pause for 1–2 seconds.

Slide 2 — Problem (0:20–0:50)
- Visual: Montage of students using laptops, quick cuts.
- On-screen bullets (max 3): "AI tools are widely accessible", "Usage ranges from study aid to assignment generation", "Institutions have limited policy guidance"
- Speaker notes: Explain the observed problem briefly: frequency of use, variety of tools, uncertainty around policy. Keep to 20–30 seconds.

Slide 3 — Why it matters (0:50–1:10)
- Visual: Split-screen of student looking confused vs student confidently studying.
- On-screen: two quick stats (if available) or short phrases: "Learning vs. Shortcut", "Academic integrity at stake"
- Speaker notes: Link the problem to real consequences — learning outcomes, assessment fairness, and future skills.

Slide 4 — What I'll do (1:10–1:40)
- Visual: Simple roadmap graphic (Interviews → Campus B-roll → Screen captures → Expert view → Solutions)
- On-screen bullets: "Student stories", "Lecturer perspectives", "Policy & solutions"
- Speaker notes: Describe the planned methodology — interviews, B-roll, screen recordings, and an optional interactive quiz. Mention planned runtime (5–10 min) and deliverables (final film, social cut, captions).

Slide 5 — Access & feasibility (1:40–2:10)
- Visual: Photos or icons for crew, gear, permissions.
- On-screen bullets: "4–6 interviews", "2 lecture halls for B-roll", "Minimal equipment (phone + external mic OK)"
- Speaker notes: Reassure the class that the topic is practical: easy access to interviewees, local relevance, minimal gear. List quick permissions needed.

Slide 6 — Call to action & ask (2:10–3:00)
- Visual: Poster thumbnail + teaser still
- On-screen text: "Feedback requested: angle, interviewees, data sources"
- Speaker notes: Close by asking for class feedback and any suggestions for interview subjects or local examples. State your name and contact.

Delivery tips
- Rehearse to fit within 3 minutes; time each slide.
- Use short, punchy sentences and keep eye contact with camera/class.
- Have the poster or 20–30s teaser ready to show after the pitch.
