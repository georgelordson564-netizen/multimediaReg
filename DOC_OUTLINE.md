# Detailed Documentary Outline

Title: AI in the Classroom: Learning or Shortcut?
Target runtime: 7:00–8:00 (editable to 5–10 mins)

Total structure (timings approximate)
- 00:00–00:20 — Cold open / Hook (visual + line)
- 00:20–01:00 — Introduce thesis and set stakes
- 01:00–03:00 — Student stories & use-cases (two short vignettes)
- 03:00–04:30 — Lecturer observations and examples
- 04:30–05:30 — Institutional perspective / policy (admin or integrity rep)
- 05:30–06:30 — Expert perspective & risks / outcomes
- 06:30–07:30 — Solutions, best practices & call to action
- 07:30–08:00 — End card (credits, resources, where to find more)

Scene-by-scene detail

00:00–00:20 — Hook
- Visual: quick cut: student typing a prompt; text appears on screen from the chatbot; student reads and nods.
- Audio: a single line: "Is AI helping us learn, or is it replacing our thinking?"
- Purpose: Grab attention and set question.

00:20–01:00 — Intro / Stakes
- VO (narration): Brief intro to the prevalence of AI tools and the central question.
- Visual: campus establishing shots, students on laptops, quick overlay stats (if available).

01:00–03:00 — Student vignette A (60s)
- Introduce Student A: name, program, year (on-screen lower third).
- Scene: student preparing an essay using AI (screen capture of a benign use-case like brainstorming or summarizing).
- Interview soundbite: "I use AI to..."
- B-roll: library, handwriting notes, laptop close-ups.
- Purpose: show everyday use and benefits.

01:40–03:00 — Student vignette B (40s)
- Student B describes a time they used AI to complete an assignment or to shortcut work.
- Include counterpoint: maybe they realized it didn’t help them learn.

03:00–04:30 — Lecturer perspective
- Interview clip: lecturer describing changes in submissions or learning behaviors.
- Show anonymized examples (with permission) of suspicious work vs original work.
- B-roll: lecturer in class, slide deck, marking notes.
- Purpose: show detection challenges and pedagogical concerns.

04:30–05:30 — Institutional / policy view
- Interview with academic integrity officer or admin.
- Discuss what policies exist, enforcement challenges, and educational gaps.
- Visuals: policy documents, web pages, campus committee meeting (if available).

05:30–06:30 — Expert & consequences
- Short interview with an expert (education technologist, learning scientist) explaining cognitive effects and long-term risks.
- Use simple animated graphic to show "Help vs Harm" spectrum.

06:30–07:30 — Solutions & practical recommendations
- Short, actionable solutions: AI literacy modules, transparent citations, redesigned assessments, honor codes, scaffolded assignments.
- Visuals: animation of a roadmap; student and lecturer agreeing on a small classroom policy.

07:30–08:00 — Closing
- VO: Restate central question and invite audience to reflect on their use of AI.
- On-screen: resources (links), credits, and call to action: "Discuss this in your next tutorial".

Editing notes
- Use short cuts and pacing to keep runtime tight.
- Use consistent lower-thirds and simple typography (Montserrat or Open Sans).
- Color grade: neutral, slightly cool for tech subjects, warm for personal stories.
- Music: unobtrusive, modern ambient bed; drop music during interviews.
- Captions: provide SRT file for accessibility.

Assets required
- Interviews: 4–6 people (2 students, 1–2 lecturers, 1 admin, 1 expert)
- B-roll: campus, libraries, lecture halls, study groups, screen captures
- Graphics: 2–3 animated overlays (roadmap, help vs harm spectrum, small charts)

Backup plan if interviews unavailable
- Use additional student voiceover narration with anonymized typed examples and public reports / articles.
